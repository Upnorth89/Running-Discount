# Fox Pro after launch: ranked roadmap (CMO, night of Nov 4, 2026)
Launch set (locked, not re-ranked): "I bought it" price protection (same-store drops, ~25 stores), next-morning drop alerts, unlimited watchlist, full price history, Founding Fox number.
Ranked by expected Pro joins + renewals per hour of Bastien's time (his part = one preview + yes/no, ~15-30 min each; the backend writes the code).

## What the evidence says before the list
- People pay for getting money back, not for seeing prices. Price history and alerts are FREE at CamelCamelCamel and Keepa (Keepa charges only for seller data). The paid consumer products are the ones that recover money: Kiko+ (Canadian Costco price-adjustment app) is $3.99/month or $39/year for automatic matching and ready-to-use claims; Pricerazzi took 15% of what it recovered. Paribus/Capital One's automatic refunds were paused or discontinued in Jan 2023 (sources disagree which), so nobody in Canada does this for running stores. Kiko+ at $39/year sits right next to our $36/$45. **So money-back features justify the price. Data features keep people renewing.**
- Our own numbers (funnel run Nov 2, Oct 19 - Nov 2): 155 real members; 41 watch something; 7 watch 6-10 items (the people the 5-item free cap touches); about 1 visit per 2 alert emails vs about 1 per 10 Friday emails (small counts: a lead, not proof). Item-specific, timely emails pull people back about 5x better.
- Shoe-mileage tracking is crowded and mostly free: Strava already emails a "time to replace" reminder; TreadLeft, Treadly, SubRunGear and COROS do it too. Strava's API (Nov 2024 terms, 2026 capacity rules per Terra's blog): 1 athlete at first, 10 without review, more needs approval with no guaranteed timeline. A Strava sync can't be promised on a schedule.

**Tease on the Pro page as "coming in 2027": #4 "Replace my shoe"**, worded: "Coming spring 2027: tell us how much you run and we'll start watching your next pair before your shoes wear out." Honest because it needs no outside approval (no Strava) and uses data Pro already collects. Do NOT tease #1/#2 as 2027: they ship in December. Don't tease Strava, push or family: each depends on a third party or on demand we haven't seen.

## Left out on purpose
- Target price: dropped Oct (runners don't know a shoe's floor). Still no.
- Back in my size: already free on shoe pages (Oct 27). Moving it behind Pro breaks the ladder (a free thing taken away).
- Pick stores/brands: the site's filters already do it for free; nobody pays for a filter.
- Size-run alerts ("your size is running out"): real but it's urgency email; the Oct 31 no-pressure rule makes it easy to get wrong and it adds email volume for little value.
- Strava sync: API capacity 10 athletes without review, no timeline for more. Revisit only if #4 is used.
- Separate "early Black Friday/Boxing Day alerts": #2 covers it honestly; everyone sees the same store data at the same reads.

## What to measure
- Per feature: share of Pro members who used it in 30 days (claim emails sent for #1, afternoon alerts sent/opened-to-visit for #2, ledger views for #3, shoes with km set for #4).
- The one number for the roadmap: Pro joins whose join session touched the feature's page or email (ref tags: pro-claim, pro-pm, pro-ledger) + refund requests in the 30-day window (target < 15%).
- Renewal intent proxy (Jan-Nov 2027): members with at least one claim or caught drop vs none.

## Stop rule
- #1: if fewer than 2 "lower elsewhere" claims qualify across all Pro purchases by Jan 31 (too few matching policies or exact matches), stop extending it and say so on the Pro page; keep same-store protection only.
- #2: if the afternoon pass produces under 5 extra alerts across all Pro members by Jan 15, keep it running (it costs nothing) but stop using it as a selling line.
- Everything ranked 3-7 waits until Pro has 20+ paying members; below that by Jan 31 (the target was 8-20), build nothing new and spend the time on getting members (shoe pages + Google), not features.
